ÌBÁ • ÀṢẸ • ÀṢÀ YORÙBÁ

Ilé Àwọn Òrìṣà. Àṣẹ tí ó ń gbé ayé.

Wọ inú ilé ìmọ̀ Òrìṣà Yorùbá — a living archive of sacred powers, deified ancestors, shrines, sacred places, ritual objects, oral memory and regional lineages. This house begins with 21 documentary entries and remains open to the many other Òrìṣà preserved across Yoruba communities.

Àwọn Òrìṣà • Ilé ìtàn àti àwòrán

Wọlé. Wo. Ka ìtàn.

Each entry begins with a real photographed shrine, staff, monument, river, grove, ritual object or festival representation and opens into a fuller documentary with place, lineage and references.

Ìkìlọ̀ / archive scope: Yoruba sacred tradition is not a fixed list. Ilé-Ifẹ̀, Ọ̀yọ́, Ìjẹ̀ṣà, Ẹ̀gbá, Ìjẹ̀bú, Ekiti, Ketu, Ondo and other communities preserve local sacred histories, titles and manifestations. Ìyámi Àjẹ́ / Àwọn Ìyá is included among the sacred powers here, while classification can differ by lineage and scholarship.
ÌMỌ̀ ÒRÌṢÀ

Ìmọ̀ Òrìṣà ń gbé inú àṣà.

The archive treats Òrìṣà as living Yoruba traditions carried by people, place, shrine, priesthood, family, praise poetry and material culture—not as fantasy characters detached from Yoruba life.

Àwọn ibi mímọ́Shrines, rivers, groves, roads, farms, royal centers and other sacred landscapes.
Oríkì & oral memoryPraise language, narratives and remembered histories shape sacred identity.
Ìdílé & lineagePriestly and family traditions preserve local emphases and regional variation.
Àṣà & ohun ìṣẹ̀ṣeStaffs, beads, iron, cloth, water, herbs, sculpture, masks and festival forms.
