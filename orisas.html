Ìṣẹ̀ṣe • Àwọn Òrìṣà • Sacred powers • Documentary archive

Òrìṣà are far more than thirteen.

This expanded archive begins with 21 major and regionally important Òrìṣà, deified ancestors and sacred-power complexes. It is intentionally not presented as an exhaustive canon: Yoruba towns, lineages and historical periods preserve many more names, titles and local manifestations.

Expanded documentary directory

Major & regional Òrìṣà.

Every card uses a real photographed shrine, ritual object, monument or documented cultural representation rather than a fantasy “face of the deity.” Open any card for a full documentary.

Archive scope: This site now includes a broader starting set, but it still does not claim that Yoruba religion has only this number of Òrìṣà. Ilé-Ifẹ̀, Ọ̀yọ́, Ìjẹ̀ṣà, Ẹ̀gbá, Ìjẹ̀bú, Ekiti, Ketu, Ondo and other communities preserve local sacred histories and names. Ìyámi Àjẹ́ / Àwọn Ìyá is included here among the sacred powers, while the page also notes that classification can differ by lineage and scholarship.
How the archive works

Picture, place, oral memory, lineage.

The documentary pages combine real visual culture with historical context, material representation, sacred geography, lineage variation and selected references.

◎Real visual evidenceShrines, staffs, carvings, monuments, rivers, masks and festival representation.
◈Oral traditionNames and meanings are treated as living traditions rather than one fixed internet chart.
✦Regional variationLocal lineages may differ in genealogy, iconography, ritual language and emphasis.
❖ReferencesEach documentary includes selected scholarly, museum or heritage references.
