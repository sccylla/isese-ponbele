Ìṣẹ̀ṣe • Àwọn Òrìṣà • Yoruba sacred worldview

Meet the Òrìṣà.

Òrìṣà are sacred powers, personalities and presences within Yoruba religious life. Their identities are preserved through oral literature, shrines, festivals, priestly lineages, art, place, kingship and everyday moral imagination. This page introduces major Òrìṣà without reducing them to simple “gods of” labels.

Major Òrìṣà and sacred powers.

Descriptions differ by town, lineage and historical period. These cards give broad cultural orientation, not a substitute for lineage-specific teaching.

✣
Wisdom • Ifá

Òrúnmìlà

Closely associated with Ifá divination, wisdom, counsel and knowledge of destiny. In many traditions he is described as witness to the choosing of destiny.

Ifá • knowledge • destiny
╳
Crossroads • mediation

Èṣù

A complex mediator of communication, consequence, exchange and the crossroads. Èṣù is not the Christian devil; that equation is a historical mistranslation.

message • choice • consequence
⚒
Iron • work • roads

Ògún

Associated with iron, technology, labour, hunting, roads and the dangerous creative power of tools. Ògún embodies disciplined force and responsibility.

iron • craft • disciplined force
≈
River • beauty • diplomacy

Ọ̀ṣun

River Òrìṣà of Ọ̀ṣogbo and beyond, associated with fertility, beauty, social grace, healing, prosperity and the intelligence of diplomacy.

river • fertility • grace
ϟ
Thunder • kingship

Ṣàngó

Linked with thunder, lightning, royal power, justice, charisma and the historical memory of Ọ̀yọ́ kingship. His traditions unite politics, ritual and performance.

thunder • justice • authority
◯
Creation • white cloth

Ọbàtálá

Also called Òrìṣà-nlá in many settings, associated with creation, whiteness, calmness, moral restraint and the shaping of human form.

creation • clarity • restraint
〰
Water • motherhood

Yemọja

A major water and motherhood figure. Yoruba and diaspora traditions differ: riverine associations in Yorubaland expanded into powerful oceanic identities abroad.

water • motherhood • lineage
↻
Wind • transformation

Ọya

Associated with winds, storms, change and the Niger River in many traditions. Her narratives often emphasize speed, courage and radical transformation.

wind • river • change
❧
Leaves • medicine

Ọ̀sányìn

Guardian of herbal and leaf knowledge in many Yoruba accounts. His presence emphasizes that sacred medicine depends on knowledge, language and correct relationship with plants.

ewé • healing • forest knowledge
∿
Deep water • mystery

Olókun

Associated with deep water, wealth, depth and hidden abundance. Gender and attributes vary significantly across Yoruba and diaspora traditions.

depth • wealth • mystery
⌁
Farm • land

Òrìṣà Oko

Linked with agriculture, cultivation, fertility of the land and the social value of farming. His traditions connect sacred order with food and productive labour.

land • farming • abundance
⌖
River • hunting • healing

Erinlẹ̀

A riverine and hunting power with important local traditions, especially around Ilobu and related areas. Associations include healing, water and the forest.

river • hunter • healing
How to understand Òrìṣà

More than a list of functions.

Yoruba sacred knowledge is relational. Òrìṣà are understood through stories, praise poetry, places, festivals, priesthoods, family histories and moral expectations.

◎PlaceRivers, towns, groves, farms, roads and royal centers shape how Òrìṣà are known.
◈Oral literatureOríkì, Ifá verses, songs and narratives preserve character and memory.
✦LineagePriestly and family traditions transmit knowledge with local variation.
❖FestivalMusic, dance, costume, procession and public memory give sacred ideas social form.
ÌBÁ

Respect regional variation.

There is no single universal chart that fixes every Òrìṣà to one colour, one day, one symbol or one personality. Ilé-Ifẹ̀, Ọ̀yọ́, Ìjẹ̀ṣà, Ẹ̀gbá, Ìjẹ̀bú, Ondo, Ekiti, Ketu, diaspora communities and individual lineages may preserve different emphases.
