Àwọn Òrìṣà • Documentary directory

Real images. Deeper histories.

Each entry uses a documented shrine, ritual object, monument, river, grove or festival representation and opens into a twelve-chapter documentary covering oral tradition, worship, history, sacred geography, diaspora and sources.

Archive scope: Yoruba sacred tradition is not a fixed list. Ilé-Ifẹ̀, Ọ̀yọ́, Ìjẹ̀ṣà, Ẹ̀gbá, Ìjẹ̀bú, Ekiti, Ketu, Ondo and other communities preserve local sacred histories, titles and manifestations. Ìyámi Àjẹ́ / Àwọn Ìyá is included among the sacred powers here, while classification can differ by lineage and scholarship.
ÌMỌ̀ ÒRÌṢÀ

Context before decoration.

The archive treats Òrìṣà as living Yoruba traditions carried by people, place, shrine, priesthood, family, praise poetry and material culture—not as fantasy characters detached from Yoruba life.
