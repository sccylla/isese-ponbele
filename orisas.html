Ilé àwọn Òrìṣà.

This archive presents major and regionally important Òrìṣà, deified ancestors and sacred-power complexes through real photographs, sacred places, ritual objects, oral memory and documented cultural context. The archive begins with 21 entries and remains open to the many other Òrìṣà preserved by Yoruba towns and lineages.

Àwọn Òrìṣà • documentary directory

Enter the sacred archive.

Each carved-style card opens a full documentary containing real cultural imagery, material representation, sacred geography, regional variation and selected references.

Ìkìlọ̀ / archive note: Yoruba sacred tradition is not a fixed list. Ilé-Ifẹ̀, Ọ̀yọ́, Ìjẹ̀ṣà, Ẹ̀gbá, Ìjẹ̀bú, Ekiti, Ketu, Ondo and other communities preserve local sacred histories, titles and manifestations. Ìyámi Àjẹ́ / Àwọn Ìyá is included among the sacred powers here, while its classification can differ by lineage and scholarship.
Ìmọ̀ Òrìṣà

Picture, place, praise, lineage.

The archive treats Òrìṣà as living Yoruba sacred traditions—known through shrine, landscape, festival, oral literature, priesthood, family history and material culture.

◎Àwọn ibi mímọ́Shrines, rivers, groves, royal centers, roads, farms and ritual spaces.
◈Oríkì & oral memoryPraise language, narratives and remembered histories shape sacred identity.
✦Ìdílé & lineagePriestly and family traditions preserve local emphases and variations.
❖Àṣà & representationStaffs, masks, beads, iron, cloth, water, herbs, sculpture and festival forms.
