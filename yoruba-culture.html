Yoruba Culture and Living Heritage

Yoruba culture is a broad living heritage spanning language, family, history, art, music, religion, philosophy, festivals, food, dress, naming traditions and social institutions across West Africa and the diaspora.

Guide

The Yoruba world

Yoruba communities are historically concentrated in southwestern Nigeria and neighboring parts of Benin and Togo, with major diasporic communities across the Atlantic world and beyond. Yoruba culture is not a single frozen set of customs: local histories and dialect regions have shaped distinct practices while maintaining many shared cultural forms.

Guide

Language, names, oríkì and oral memory

The Yoruba language carries philosophy, humor, proverbs, praise poetry, ritual language and historical memory. Personal names often express circumstances, values, family histories or hopes. Oríkì—praise poetry and praise names—can preserve personal, family, town and Òrìṣà identities.

Our Yoruba dictionary is designed as an entry point into this vocabulary and its cultural context.

Guide

Ifá, Òrìṣà and indigenous religion

Yoruba indigenous religion includes diverse traditions associated with Ìṣẹ̀ṣe, Ifá, Òrìṣà, ancestors and sacred places. Religion is one part of Yoruba culture, not a synonym for Yoruba identity as a whole.

The Òrìṣà directory documents figures such as Òrúnmìlà, Èṣù, Ògún, Ọ̀ṣun, Ṣàngó, Ọbàtálá and Yemọja with attention to regional variation.

Guide

Art, performance and festivals

Yoruba visual and performance traditions include carving, textiles, beadwork, metalwork, masquerade, drumming, dance and festival arts. Sacred and civic festivals can bring together religion, kingship, lineage memory, music, costume and public space.

Objects and performances should be understood within the communities and institutions that give them meaning, rather than as anonymous “African art.”

Guide

Culture across the diaspora

Atlantic histories carried Yoruba people, language, religious concepts and artistic forms into the Americas and Caribbean. Yoruba-derived traditions developed in places such as Cuba and Brazil, creating new institutions while preserving significant continuities with West African sources.

Isese Ponbele distinguishes between West African Yoruba contexts and later diasporic developments when those distinctions matter.
