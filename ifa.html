Ifá: Yoruba Divination and Knowledge System

Ifá is a Yoruba divination, literary and knowledge system associated with Òrúnmìlà and interpreted by trained specialists through an extensive corpus known as Odù Ifá.

Guide

What is Ifá?

Ifá is one of the best-known Yoruba systems of divination and knowledge transmission. UNESCO describes it as a system of signs interpreted by a trained diviner and supported by an extensive literary corpus. It is practiced among Yoruba communities and in parts of the African diaspora.

Calling Ifá only “fortune telling” misses its literary, ethical, historical and interpretive dimensions.

Guide

Òrúnmìlà and the Odù Ifá corpus

Òrúnmìlà is closely associated with Ifá and is widely understood as a divinity of wisdom and divinatory knowledge. The corpus is organized around 256 Odù. Each Odù is associated with a sign and a body of verses, narratives, proverbs and interpretive material transmitted through training.

The content and recitation of verses can vary by lineage and region, which is one reason serious study depends on context and apprenticeship.

Guide

Babaláwo, Ọ̀pẹ̀lẹ̀ and ikin

A Babaláwo is a trained Ifá priest or diviner. Depending on community and lineage, consultation may involve sacred palm nuts known as ikin or the divination chain called Ọ̀pẹ̀lẹ̀. The ọ̀pọ́n Ifá is the divination tray associated with many Ifá practices.

These objects are parts of a larger institution of learning and ritual discipline; they should not be treated as isolated props.

Guide

Ifá within Yoruba culture and Ìṣẹ̀ṣe

Ifá exists inside a broader Yoruba cultural and religious world. It intersects with Ìṣẹ̀ṣe, Òrìṣà traditions, language, praise poetry, concepts of destiny, family institutions and community history.

At the same time, not every Yoruba person practices Ifá, and Yoruba identity should never be reduced to one religion.

Sources

UNESCO recognition

UNESCO inscribed the Ifá divination system on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2008, following its earlier proclamation as a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity.
