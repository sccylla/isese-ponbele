Odundun

Never-die / Kalanchoe
Kalanchoe crenata (Andrews) Haw.
High confidence
CrassulaceaeSucculent subshrubTropical Africa

A fleshy-leaved succulent with rounded to crenate leaf margins. Kew lists the species as native across tropical and southern Africa and the Arabian Peninsula.

Identification: Thick, succulent leaves with visibly scalloped edges are a useful visual feature.

Ewuro

Bitter leaf
Gymnanthemum amygdalinum (syn. Vernonia amygdalina)
High confidence
AsteraceaeShrub / small treeTropical Africa

The plant commonly known as bitter leaf. Kew currently accepts the name Gymnanthemum amygdalinum, with Vernonia amygdalina treated as a synonym.

Identification: Leaves are generally elliptic to lanceolate with toothed or serrated margins; the plant grows as a branching shrub or small tree.

Iroko

African iroko
Milicia excelsa (Welw.) C.C.Berg
High confidence
MoraceaeLarge treeTropical Africa

A major tropical African tree in the mulberry family. Kew records Milicia excelsa as native across tropical Africa, including Benin and Nigeria.

Identification: Mature leaves are usually elliptic to oblong with a pointed apex. The species can become a very large forest tree.

Atare

Grains of paradise / Melegueta pepper
Aframomum melegueta K.Schum.
High confidence
ZingiberaceaeRhizomatous herbWest Tropical Africa

A ginger-family plant native from West Tropical Africa to Angola. Kew lists “grains of paradise” among its English common names.

Identification: Leafy shoots carry narrow elliptic leaves; the plant grows from rhizomes and its fruits contain the aromatic seeds commonly associated with Atare.

Tete

Amaranth / African spinach group
Amaranthus spp. — representative image: Amaranthus hybridus
Locality dependent
AmaranthaceaeAnnual herbSpecies varies

Tete is used for amaranth-type leafy vegetables, but the exact botanical species can vary by locality. The photograph here is a representative Amaranthus hybridus, not a claim that every plant called Tete is that species.

Identification: Confirm the local plant before assigning a species name. Amaranthus hybridus is an erect annual with ovate to lanceolate leaves.
